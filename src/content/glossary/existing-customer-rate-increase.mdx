---
term: "Existing customer rate increase (ECRI)"
definition: "An existing customer rate increase, or ECRI, is a scheduled increase in the monthly rent charged to a tenant who is already renting a unit, typically sent as a mailed or emailed notice ahead of the increase taking effect. Storage operators use ECRIs, often every six to twelve months, to move a tenant's rate toward the current street rate over time, since new tenants are usually acquired at a lower promotional rate than long-term tenants end up paying. ECRIs are one of the biggest levers on revenue per unit, but set too aggressively or too often, they also drive the move-outs a facility's marketing then has to replace."
example: "A tenant who moved in at $99 a month receives an ECRI notice raising the rate to $119, still below the current street rate."
